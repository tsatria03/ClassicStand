Design and balance ideas from real **player feedback (2026-08-20, v3.3)** by a blind player who praised the game's depth and accessibility. These are **suggestions, not bugs** — nothing here is scheduled or decided yet. Confirmed bugs from the same feedback (cups, spoiled-item wipe, serve-all groups) are in [[project_known_player_bugs]]; the menu refactor is [[project_menu_refactor_plan]].

**Recipe / ingredients:**
- **Quality feedback is confusing because it's ratio-based. — ✓ partly addressed in 3.5 (orig #12): the mild feedback lines now name the culprit ingredient and the imbalance (e.g. "too much lemon for the sugar"); still ratio-based underneath, which is acceptable.** `get_customer_feedback` judges on ratios, so lowering one ingredient raises the others' ratios and triggers "too sweet/too sour" even though you *reduced* something — the player can't tell whether sugar or lemons specifically need adjusting. Consider naming the offending ingredient, or moving away from a pure-ratio model.
- **Salt and ice feel inert. — DONE in 3.6 (Direction 2, weather-driven), design in [[project_plan_salt_ice_upside]].** They only ever produce "too salty/too icy" complaints — no upside. Player's principle: every ingredient should give both a benefit and a drawback. Agreed direction (2026-08-21): ice = hot-day refreshment bonus (cold-day penalty), salt = universal flavor enhancer with a hot-day electrolyte bump; bonus plugs into the good-drink tip/rep path and must be spoken; all thresholds/magnitudes config-tunable.
- **Spoilage is too fast. — PLANNED (Direction A, per-ingredient upgrades + base-rate softening), design in [[project_plan_storage_upgrades]]; not yet coded.** Water evaporation, salt clumping, and ice melting happen so quickly they're hard to manage (ice loses 50–100% nightly). Agreed fix (2026-08-21): soften the base rates (move them to config) AND add per-ingredient tiered preservation upgrades (icebox/pantry/sealed jars/covered jugs) that multiply the overnight loss fraction down.

**Customers:**
- **Talking is almost always negative — all risk, no reward. — DONE in 3.6 (Direction B, type-specific payoffs), design in [[project_plan_rewarding_talk]]; the calm-a-mean sliver ships with the mean feature.** Agreed direction (2026-08-21): chatting below the anger limit builds rapport → tip/rep bonus at serve; chatters (elderly/nice/returning) reward it, prickly types (mean/critic) risk it but have a small chance to be calmed (calm mechanic shared with [[project_plan_mean_customer_consequences]]); config-tunable; every rapport/calm event must be spoken. In the talk handler (`customer.nvgt:629–659`): under the per-type limit (`get_talk_limit`, 2–5 lines) talking just speaks the next scripted line and does `talk_count++` — no mechanical effect; at/over the limit the customer walks off angry with a rep penalty (`1.0 + daynumber/10`, `:643`). There is **no positive outcome coded**: talking never touches `patiencetimer`, never improves the drink reaction, tip chance, or mood — outcomes are only neutral (hear a line) or negative (walk-off). The scripted lines in `customers.table [speeches]` are also written to escalate by mood (normal: happy→fine→restless→angry; even nice trends to restless/rushed), so it *feels* like they're losing patience. Net: a cliff with no upside, so a rational player stops talking entirely. Options to discuss: a chance talking calms an angry/mean customer or lifts mood a tier; extend/refresh patience on a good beat; small tip-chance or reaction bonus for polite engagement; type-specific payoffs (e.g. elderly reward chatting, mean stays risky).
- **Rude-customer consequences feel absent. — DONE in 3.6 (Direction B, social ripple, fully tunable), design in [[project_plan_mean_customer_consequences]]; includes the talk-calm counterplay.** Agreed direction (2026-08-21): remove the mean pay-anyway exemption (`:413`), bigger rep hit, and a config-tunable "badmouth" ripple that saps nearby waiting customers' patience / brief rep dip on a bad mean interaction; talk-calming (shared with [[project_plan_rewarding_talk]]) converts a calmed mean to a normal outcome as the counterplay; every consequence must be spoken. The flavor implies a threat — mean arrival "stomping…bad mood" (`customer.nvgt:63`), hostile dialogue (`:240`), short fuse (2 talk lines, patience ×0.7 in `customers.table`). But mechanically a mean customer ≈ a normal no-tipper: `bad=true` always (`:369`/`:764`), yet they still **pay even on a spill** — mean is explicitly exempt from the cash/sold-cup loss at `:414` — take only the small rep hit (`0.5 + daynumber/20`), and never tip (`tip_chance=0`, `:459`/`:801`). Net: a mean customer is *less* punishing than a normal customer served a genuinely bad drink (who refuses to pay AND takes a bigger rep hit). The only place rudeness bites is overpricing (1.5× refuse chance, `:302`). Options to discuss when we get to it: a larger reputation hit; sour nearby customers' patience/mood; a chance to badmouth the stand (temporary wave/rep dip); or remove the mean-pays-anyway exemption at `:414`.
- **Napkin / greeter rework. — napkins-purchasable half ✓ DONE in 3.5** (new Cleaning station in the market at 37,25 sells single napkins + small/medium/large packs, via `single_napkins.store` / `bundle_napkins.store`); the greeter-relocation half was set aside. Rather than a greeter permanently standing at the stand delivering help text, move that help into a tutorial / help system / README backstory, and make **napkins a purchasable supply** like other inventory. (Overlaps the napkin-persistence bug and the greeter's permanent-text coupling noted elsewhere.)

**Economy / balance:**
- **Posters feel pointless next to the viral bonus. — ✓ DONE in 3.5 (approach C, config-only).** The `lucky_bonus` event is now gated on `condition=posters` and its payout scales by `placed` (`20*placed*level*day` to `80*placed*level*day` in `main.event`), so the viral windfall is earned by having posters up rather than arriving free. Original finding: The `lucky_bonus` event pays `100–500 × level × day` cash (`main.event`), dwarfing poster value and letting players handle huge waves without marketing. Posters, by contrast, only add `placed_posters × 0.75` customers per wave plus a patience bonus. Rebalance one against the other — e.g. cap/curve the viral payout, or make posters compound (bigger wave/patience/tip effect) so marketing is a real investment.
- **Per-cup and per-group pricing.** Groups should pay per cup (ties to the confirmed serve-all bug). Player also wants the option to set different prices for different customer groups — e.g. discounts for children or the elderly. **NOT config-possible today** (checked 2026-08-20): selling is a single global `selling_price` applied uniformly (`customer.nvgt:349`); the only per-type price difference is HARDCODED (desperate ×1.5, child ×0.6, `customer.nvgt:350–351`); `customers.table` has no price field and the `.store` files are buy-costs only. Split into two todo entries: (1) **config path — ✓ DONE in 3.5** — implemented as a new `[prices]` section in `customers.table` (`type=multiplier`), loaded via `get_customer_price_multiplier` and applied in both serve paths (readme documents it); replaced the hardcoded desperate/child numbers; (2) **player-facing feature (real code) — PLANNED, design in [[project_plan_per_group_pricing]]; not yet coded.** A new "Pricing station" map menu (Direction A, % of base) with Customer prices + Sale price, replacing the forced daily price popup (base carries over clamped); refuse/patience become per-group; applied in both serve and serve-all (the serve-all group bug was fixed in 3.4). Chosen 2026-08-21.
- **Day segments feel too short. — ✓ DONE in 3.5 (all three sections).** Shipped: (1) the economy is decoupled from the raw `level` via `economy_level()` (`level * 6.0 / maxlevel`, anchored to the historical 6-level day) applied to cup price, event/thief amounts, wave growth, patience, and all store buy-costs — identical at maxlevel 6; (2) `hours_per_level` is now a config value in `main.table [time]` (must divide 24 evenly, silent fallback to 4) that derives `maxlevel` and the `clocktime` step, documented in readme; (3) wave sizes raised in `customers.table [waves]` (`base_min` 1→3, `base_max` 5→8, `max_customers` 20→28) so early periods open with a real line rather than clearing instantly. Known trade-off (not solved): total per-day demand is not normalized across level counts — coarser day settings yield fewer, individually-bigger periods. Original finding: A day = 6 periods (`level` 1→6, `maxlevel=6`); a period has NO fixed length — it ends when its wave clears (`all_done`, `customer.nvgt:953–961`), then a 10s countdown auto-advances (`:946`; N skips). Early waves are tiny (~1–6 customers at level 1, `:886–918`), so periods end almost instantly and six fly by. Partly config-tunable now (raise `base_min`/`base_max`/`max_customers` in `customers.table [waves]`); fuller fixes need code — a minimum period duration, spreading customer arrivals over time, or a longer post-wave countdown. **Related open idea (under discussion, not yet scoped): make hours-per-level / levels-per-day configurable** (currently hardcoded `clocktime += 4` × 6 levels = 24h). CAVEAT: `level` does double duty — it's both the time-of-day index AND an economy scalar (cup pricing `scale = level*daynumber` in `calculate_cup_price`; event amounts and thief cash use `level`), so changing levels-per-day rescales the whole economy unless that coupling is decoupled first. Also must divide 24 evenly.
  **Decision 2026-08-20 — Option A (keep the through-the-day ramp), decouple first. Both steps now in the todo.** Plan, in order: (1) **Decouple** — economy scales on `daynumber` + a **normalized time-of-day fraction** (`level / maxlevel`, 0→1 across a day) instead of raw `level`; this preserves today's rising-through-the-day feel but makes it independent of granularity. Touch: `calculate_cup_price`, the `main.event` amount expressions + thief cash, wave scaling (`extra = min(extra_max, level-1)` and the patience `level*7000` term), and the modding docs (the `level` expression variable no longer means raw period count — see [[project_data_driven_config]]). (2) **Then** make `hours_per_level` (equivalently `levels_per_day`) a config value that derives `maxlevel` and the `clocktime` step (currently hardcoded `clocktime += 4` × 6 = 24h); validate it divides 24 evenly. Do NOT do (2) before (1). **Sequencing (dev decision 2026-08-21):** the day-segment-length tuning (orig todo #3, bumping wave sizes) is deferred to be done together with / after the decouple → configurable-hours work — wave size scales on `level` (`extra = min(extra_max, level-1)` in `spawn_customer_wave`), so the ideal wave numbers depend on the final levels-per-day count. Tuning it earlier would likely need re-tuning.

**UX / audio polish:**
- **Movement friction.** Tile-based movement forces constant coordinate re-checking to reposition; the action station and customer counter feel farther apart than necessary, slowing the loop. **DESIGN NOTE ONLY — deliberately NOT added to the todo (dev decision 2026-08-20).** Reason: the player gave no concrete target for how close the two stations should be. The layout IS measurable now: the plaintext layout is available as the committed master `cst/data/maps/neighborhood.map.txt` (commented, 50×50 — see [[project_save_data_layout]]). Actual geometry — actions station `menu 2 6 19 31 actsmenu` (far west of the stand), customer counter tile (14,25) (east edge); the stand spans x0–15,y18–32, so the two are ~8 tiles apart at opposite ends of the same building. If revisited: (a) station spacing is a small map edit (e.g. shift the `actsmenu` rectangle east toward x8–12, or nudge the counter west); (b) the "constant coordinate re-checking" half is broader navigation UX and would be code (e.g. an objective audio beacon), not a tile move.
- **The next-cycle clock sound is too loud and repetitive.** The **loudness** is an asset-only fix the dev owns — lower the gain and re-export the .ogg, no code change (mind the filename-based preload cache when overwriting: [[project_nvgt_sound_preload_cache]]). The **"repetitive"** part is NOT a code loop: `timestart.ogg` and `timestop.ogg` are one-shot `p.play_stationary(..., false)` cues fired once at each wave start/end (`customer.nvgt:885` / `:967`); there is no looping tick anywhere. What the player perceived is just frequency — you hear the start/stop pair up to ~6 times per day (once per period), which is why they bundled it with "segments feel quite short." **Dev decision (2026-08-20): keep it — the cue is intentional as a shift start/end marker, so the repetition is by design (effectively wontfix). Only the volume asset tweak remains.**

Overall the player called it a rare, impressively deep, genuinely accessible audio business sim — the items above are refinements, not complaints about the core.
